Legal, ethics and safety

Cloning your own voice is legal almost everywhere. The questions that remain are: what you must tell your audience, what you hand over to the vendor, how you keep the clone from being used against you, and where the line is with other people's voices.

Not legal advice These are research notes for a tutorial, read on 2026-10-17. Laws differ by country and change often. For paid courses, company training, or anything involving other people's voices, check with a qualified lawyer and, in a company, with the data protection officer and works council.
Bottom line
  1. Disclose every time. One spoken sentence at the start ("This narration uses an AI-generated version of my voice"), a visible label on video, and a line in the description. That covers the EU AI Act, Chinese rules, TikTok, Udemy and LinkedIn at almost no cost. Inference
  2. Read the licence you grant the vendor. ElevenLabs, Cartesia and Fish Audio take broad, mostly perpetual licences to your recordings; Microsoft says it does not train on them. Verified
  3. Turn off voice-ID at your bank once a clone of you exists (or once you have published a lot of clean audio). Inference, based on documented bypasses
  4. Never clone anyone else without explicit, written, purpose-specific consent. Verified (vendor policies, court cases)

Rights in your own voice

EU: GDPR

Your voice is personal data. The EDPB calls voice data "inherently biometric"; processing it to identify you is special-category data under Art. 9. Whether a TTS voice model (built to generate, not identify) is Art. 9 data is unsettled; Resemble treats it as such. Safest: treat clone data as sensitive. Verified · classification Inference

Germany

No special voice statute; the voice is protected by the general personality right. LG Berlin II, 20 Aug 2025 (2 O 202/24): an AI imitation of a well-known dubbing voice in commercial YouTube videos was treated like a human impersonation; €4,000 notional licence fee. Unverified (secondary reports of the judgment)

China

Beijing Internet Court, 23 Apr 2024: a person's voice rights extend to an AI voice that the public can identify by timbre, intonation and style. Owning the copyright in recordings did not include the right to clone the voice. ¥250,000 damages. Verified (court's English page)

United States

Right of publicity is state law. Tennessee's ELVIS Act (in force 1 Jul 2024) explicitly protects voice, including simulations. The federal NO FAKES Act is not law: stalled in the Senate after an objection on 30 Sep 2026. Verified (Act text, Congress.gov) · stall Unverified (press)

Two practical lessons
  • A recording contract is not a cloning licence (Beijing case). Do not build your clone from studio or employer recordings someone else holds rights to; record a dedicated set yourself (page 6). Inference
  • If the clone is made at work, for company training, on company accounts, the company is the deployer and the data controller. Agree in writing: scope, duration, who may generate audio with it, and deletion when you leave. Inference

EU AI Act Article 50: is your narration a "deep fake"?

The rules and dates

  • Art. 50(4): deployers of an AI system that generates audio "constituting a deep fake" must disclose that it is artificially generated. Art. 3(60) defines a deep fake as AI-generated audio (or image, video) that "resembles existing persons … and would falsely appear to a person to be authentic or truthful". Verified
  • Applies from 2 August 2026. The Digital Omnibus (in force 27 Jul 2026) delayed only the provider duty to machine-mark outputs (Art. 50(2)) to 2 December 2026, and only for systems already on the market. The deployer disclosure duty was not delayed. Verified (Commission FAQ, 24 Jul 2026, read by the main agent)
  • Personal use is excluded, but "professional" is broad. The Commission's guidelines say any activity giving "an economic benefit on a regular basis" counts as professional. A monetised channel or a paid course is professional. Verified
  • Machine-readable marks are not enough for deployers; the disclosure must be perceivable by people. Fines up to €15 M or 3 % of worldwide turnover. Verified

Applying it to a tutorial in your own voice

  1. Resembles an existing person? Yes, that is the point of a clone. The Commission's guidelines (20 Jul 2026) name "voice" explicitly.
  2. Would it falsely appear authentic? This is the deciding question. Authenticity covers the "creation process (including … the involvement of real human beings)". A listener who hears your voice assumes you recorded it, even when every word is yours. The guidelines' closest example, a newspaper podcast with cloned voices of its regular presenters, is classed as a deep fake; so is a synthetic CEO avatar congratulating employees, so internal company content is not exempt.
  3. Exceptions? The creative/artistic exception excludes content that is mainly "informative or commercial", which tutorials are. The human-review exception exists only for text.
  4. Counter-argument. The guidelines say voice replication for fictional narrators is not a deep fake "when there is no deception as to the identity of the narrators". If your audience knows the narration is synthetic, there is no false appearance; the disclosure itself creates that knowledge.
Practical reading Treat cloned-voice tutorials published to EU audiences as Art. 50(4) deep fakes and disclose. The final Code of Practice on transparency (10 Jun 2026) recommends, for audio-only content, "a short audible disclaimer in plain and simple natural language … at the beginning", in the content's language or English; for video, the EU "AI" icon or equivalent. Inference (no authority has ruled on someone cloning themselves) · Code text Verified (research helper)

China: labelling rules for Mandarin content

  • Measures for Labeling AI-Generated Synthetic Content (人工智能生成合成内容标识办法), effective 1 September 2025. Audio needs an explicit label: a spoken notice or an audio-rhythm cue at the start, end or a suitable middle point; files also carry an implicit label in metadata. Art. 10: users publishing AI content must proactively declare it and use the platform's labelling function. Verified (CAC text)
  • The companion national standard (GB 45438-2025, read in its draft form) says a spoken label should contain "人工智能" or "AI" plus "生成" and/or "合成"; the rhythm cue is "短长 短短" (Morse code for "AI"). Unverified (draft text on a secondary host)
  • The 2023 Deep Synthesis Provisions require separate consent before editing someone's voice, and conspicuous labels for voice imitation that may confuse the public. Verified
  • A suitable opening line: "本课程旁白使用人工智能合成的本人声音。" (This course's narration uses an AI-synthesised version of my own voice.) Inference

No watermark survives every attack. A 2025 systematisation study (arXiv 2503.19176) tested 9 open schemes against 109 attack configurations: "None of the surveyed watermarking schemes is robust enough to withstand all tested distortions". RAW-Bench (arXiv 2505.19663) found neural codecs the hardest attack. Watermarks help honest attribution; they do not stop an impersonator, which is why the human-perceivable disclosure matters. Verified (paper abstracts)

Protecting your voice data

Modern zero-shot models need 5–30 seconds of clean audio. Your clean training set, your local fine-tuned model, a Google voice-cloning key or an Azure speaker profile ID is a ready-made impersonation kit. Inference

  • Storage: encrypted at rest (BitLocker on Windows); never in a git repository, a public cloud folder or on Hugging Face. Practice
  • Access: separate vendor account with passkey or app-based MFA; API keys and voice keys in a secret manager, rotated. Practice
  • Vendor settings on day 1: opt out of training, keep the clone private, use a paid tier where the free tier trains on your data. Verified (settings exist as described)
  • When you stop: delete the model, history and uploaded samples at the vendor; send a GDPR Art. 17 erasure request if needed; delete local copies. Practice
  • Reports of a large 2026 "ElevenLabs breach" on low-quality sites were not confirmed by any primary source and are excluded here. Unverified

Bank voice-ID is broken

  • Feb 2023: a Vice journalist cloned his own voice with a free tool and got into his Lloyds Bank phone account via "Voice ID" (after several attempts; date of birth also required). Verified (article)
  • 22 Jul 2025: Sam Altman at a Federal Reserve conference: institutions still accepting a voice print to move money is "a crazy thing to still be doing. AI has fully defeated that." Verified (Fed transcript)
  • Nov 2024: FinCEN alert on deepfake media used to bypass identity verification; May 2025: FBI warning about AI voice messages impersonating senior US officials. Verified
  • HSBC UK and Barclays still offer voice ID (Barclays: "you can switch it off at any time"); AWS ended Amazon Connect Voice ID on 20 May 2026. Verified
Do this Switch off voice-ID at banks, telcos and company helpdesks; use app, passkey or PIN authentication. Agree a family and team "safe word" or call-back rule. Tell your company's finance and IT teams that your voice is now easy to synthesise. Inference

What is not allowed

  • Cloning anyone without consent. Every vendor forbids it (ElevenLabs: "without their explicit and informed consent"); Azure, Google and OpenAI enforce it with a recorded consent statement. It can infringe personality rights (Berlin 2025, Beijing 2024, ELVIS Act) and breach GDPR. Verified
  • Germany, pending: draft § 201b StGB (cabinet 7 Oct 2026) would criminalise distributing deceptive deep fakes that seriously damage someone's reputation; consent makes it lawful. Not yet law. Verified (ministry press release)
  • Colleagues' voices for company training: in Germany, employee consent must be in writing or electronic, with a notice of purpose and withdrawal (§ 26 BDSG), and German data protection authorities see consent in employment as rarely valid. Plan for withdrawal and for the person leaving. Verified (law text)
  • US robocalls: AI voices count as "artificial or prerecorded voice" under the TCPA (FCC, Feb 2024); calls need prior consent. Verified
  • Vendor policies also forbid impersonating officials or candidates, political campaigning without written consent, fraud, removing watermarks, and samples of minors (ElevenLabs Prohibited Use Policy, 9 Oct 2026). Verified

Checklist

  1. Decide whose content it is: yours (you are deployer and controller) or your employer's (get it in writing).
  2. Use a paid plan for commercial use; opt out of training on day 1; read the licence you grant.
  3. Record a dedicated set yourself, for this purpose only.
  4. Encrypt and protect the recordings, models and keys like passwords.
  5. Disclose every time: spoken line at the start, visible label, description line; Udemy's exact sentence; Chinese 声明 on Chinese platforms.
  6. Keep vendor watermarks and C2PA metadata intact; optionally add AudioSeal.
  7. Never clone anyone else without written, purpose-specific consent.
  8. Turn off voice-ID; set up a safe word; brief finance and IT.
  9. When you stop: delete at the vendor, request erasure, delete local copies.
  10. Watch: § 201b StGB, NO FAKES Act, the GDPR Digital Omnibus.
