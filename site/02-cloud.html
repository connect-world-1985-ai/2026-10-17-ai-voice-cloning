Cloud voice-cloning services

Hosted services are the fastest route to a good result: upload a few minutes of yourself, call an API, get speech. The differences that matter for you are who lets an individual in, how well they do Mandarin, what they do with your voice data, and what an hour of narration costs.

Bottom line (state on 2026-10-17)
  • ElevenLabs is the most complete self-serve option for an individual: instant clone from the $6 Starter plan, professional clone (own voice only) from the $22 Creator plan, Mandarin and English on Eleven v4, good API. About $4–5 per English hour and about $1.20 per Mandarin hour at API list price. Verified (pricing page, docs)
  • Google Gemini 3.8 Flash TTS can now replicate a voice self-serve from 10–30 s plus a spoken consent clip, at about $0.82 per audio hour in any language (price doubles from 2027-01-01). Much cheaper, ranked lower on the independent cloned-voice arena. Verified (Google docs) · Experimental (Preview)
  • Gated or gone: Azure Personal Voice and Google Chirp 3 custom voice need approval; OpenAI custom voices are sales-only; Resemble's hosted platform, LMNT and PlayHT are no longer options. Verified
  • No leaderboard measures how much a clone sounds like you. You must test with your own voice (page 11). Inference

How to read the price column

All "per hour" figures are list prices for one hour of finished narration, excluding tax and one-off voice fees, computed with these assumptions:

  • English: about 9,000 words ≈ 50,000–60,000 billable characters per hour. Artificial Analysis uses 49,500; ElevenLabs' own calculator assumes 1,000 characters ≈ 1 minute. Inference
  • Mandarin: normal Mandarin speech is about 240 characters (syllables) per minute, so ≈ 15,000 billable characters per hour including punctuation. Teaching is often slower. Unverified (Chinese secondary sources on speaking rate)
  • Whether a vendor counts one Chinese character as one billable character is not always documented; the Mandarin figures assume 1:1. Fish Audio bills UTF-8 bytes (3 bytes per Chinese character). Gemini bills audio duration, so language does not matter. Unverified for most vendors

Per-service notes

ElevenLabs

  • Models (docs, 2026-10-17): eleven_v4 (90+ languages including Mandarin and Cantonese, 10,000 characters per request), eleven_v4_turbo (~100 ms latency), eleven_v3 (5,000 characters), eleven_multilingual_v2, eleven_flash_v2_5. v4 was published 2026-09-28. Verified
  • Instant clone: confirm a "right and consent" checkbox; 1–2 minutes works best; more than 3 minutes "can even be detrimental". Verified
  • Professional clone: "For now, we only allow you to clone your own voice"; you pass a voice-captcha by reading lines; 30 minutes minimum, ideally 2–3 hours. Slots: Creator/Pro 1, Scale 3, Business 10. Verified
  • Commercial use: the free plan is non-commercial and requires "elevenlabs.io" or "11.ai" in the title of published content; any paid plan includes a commercial licence, which survives cancellation for content made while subscribed. Verified (help centre)
  • Plans (monthly): Free 10k credits, Starter $6 / 30k, Creator $22 / 121k, Pro $99 / 600k, Scale $299 / 1.8M, Business $990 / 6M. The API pricing page shows Creator "275k characters of TTS (v4) included", a conflict with 121k credits. Verified, conflict unresolved
  • Data: you retain rights in your output; you grant ElevenLabs a perpetual licence to use your voice to improve and develop services, with a promise not to "commercialize your voice on a standalone basis without your permission"; opt-out of training in settings (not retroactive). Details on page 9. Verified (Terms of Use, 9 Oct 2026)
  • Listen: the Eleven v4 launch post contains a real-voice versus v4-clone pair.

Google: two different products

  • Gemini 3.8 Flash TTS voice replication (docs last updated 2026-09-24): POST /v1beta/voices with a 10–30 s reference and a consent clip of the same adult reading a fixed sentence (Chinese version: "我是此声音的拥有者并授权谷歌使用此声音创建语音合成模型"). Google rejects the request if the consent transcript does not match. Recommended format 24 kHz mono 16-bit WAV. Verified (main agent read the docs)
  • Pricing tier matters for privacy: on the Gemini API free tier, content is "used to improve our products"; on the paid tier, it is not. Do not clone your voice on the free tier. Verified (helper, Gemini pricing page)
  • Chirp 3 Instant Custom Voice is a separate Cloud TTS product: allow-listed, $60 per 1M characters, and it cannot carry an English clone into Chinese. Verified
  • Listen / try: Google AI Studio has an interactive "Voice Replication" page where you can record yourself and audition the result.

Microsoft Azure

  • Personal Voice is technically ideal (5–90 s, about 100 languages, $24 per 1M characters, watermarked output), but it is Limited Access, built for apps that let their own users clone themselves, and published content is allowed only in listed use cases such as business content and media dubbing. Whether an individual's YouTube course qualifies depends on Microsoft's approval. Verified (transparency note) · eligibility Unverified
  • Microsoft states customer training data "isn't used by Microsoft to train or improve any Microsoft text to speech voice models". Verified

Chinese vendors

  • MiniMax has a strong reputation for Mandarin and the most automation-friendly long-form API (1M characters per async job, word-level subtitles), but is the most expensive per character and documents no consent step. Its Chinese terms give you the IP in outputs but a non-commercial base licence unless paid terms say otherwise. Verified · paid commercial clause Unverified
  • Alibaba's Qwen-Audio-3.1 is #2 on the English cloned-voice board, but the international region offers only 3.0 models. Check the region before relying on that ranking. Verified
  • Volcengine (ByteDance) and Tencent are cheap and Mandarin-native but need Chinese accounts with real-name verification, and their documentation is in Chinese. Verified (Tencent); Unverified (Volcengine real-name)
  • Content published on Chinese platforms must carry AI labels since 1 Sep 2025 (page 9).

OpenAI's policy on custom voices

OpenAI previewed "Voice Engine" in 2024 and held back a broad release. As of October 2026 the API has custom-voice endpoints (/v1/audio/voice_consents, /v1/audio/voices) usable with gpt-4o-mini-tts and Realtime, but "Custom voices created from recordings are limited to eligible customers. Contact our sales team." A sample of up to 30 s and an exact consent phrase are required; at most 20 such voices per organisation. Normal developer organisations report "no access". Verified (OpenAI custom-voices guide) · access reports Unverified (community forum)
