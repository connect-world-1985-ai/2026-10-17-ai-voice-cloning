Voice conversion: speak it (or let a stock voice speak it), then make it you

Voice conversion (VC) keeps the words, timing and intonation of an input recording and swaps the timbre for yours. For teaching it is a complement to text-to-speech, not a replacement: it is how you get exactly the emphasis you want, or repair a sentence without re-recording a session.

Bottom line (October 2026)
  • For narration, VC is a second tool, used when TTS prosody is not good enough or to re-voice a stock voice. Most people should start with TTS cloning (pages 2–3) and add VC later. Inference
  • The established local route on Windows + NVIDIA is RVC or its actively maintained fork Applio (both MIT), trained on about 10–30 minutes of your voice. Verified (repositories, 2026-10-17)
  • Seed-VC, the best-known zero-shot open VC, is archived (last push 2025-04-20, GPL-3.0); so-vits-svc is archived and its fork says it is no longer maintained. Verified (GitHub API, 2026-10-17)
  • Cloud: ElevenLabs Voice Changer works with your own clone, $0.12 per minute via the API, up to 5 minutes per request. Verified (ElevenLabs API pricing and docs, read 2026-10-17)

How it works, in one picture

The diagram on page 1 shows the pipeline. In short:

  1. Content encoder. A self-supervised speech model (HuBERT, ContentVec, WavLM, or a Whisper encoder) turns the source audio into frame-by-frame features that carry what is said but little of who says it.
  2. Pitch (F0). Extracted separately (RVC uses RMVPE) so the intonation survives. It is usually shifted to your typical range.
  3. Your voice. Either a model trained on your recordings (RVC, Applio), a speaker embedding from a short reference clip (zero-shot: Seed-VC, OpenVoice, CosyVoice VC, MeanVC, Chatterbox VC), or a lookup into your reference audio (kNN-VC replaces each frame with the average of its closest matches in your recordings).
  4. Decoder and vocoder render the waveform. RVC adds a retrieval index built from your training features; at inference it blends source features with the nearest stored features of your voice, pulling the timbre closer to real you.

Sources: RVC, Applio, kNN-VC, Seed-VC READMEs (read 2026-10-17); Seed-VC paper. Verified

Three ways VC fits the teaching workflow

1. Your performance, your best voice

Record yourself reading the script quickly, on any mic, maybe on a tired day. Convert it with a model trained on your best session. You keep your own emphasis and pacing, which TTS can only guess. Practice

2. Re-voice a stock TTS voice

Generate the lesson with a stock voice whose prosody you like (for Mandarin: a native-speaker voice), then convert it to your timbre. Native pronunciation, your timbre. Inference

3. Repair a single line

A sentence in a finished video changes. Record it on your laptop mic, convert it, splice it in. ElevenLabs describes Voice Changer as "preserving the tone and delivery of the original voice", useful for fixing pronunciations. Verified (ElevenLabs docs)

The accent comes from the source, not from you

VC changes timbre, not pronunciation. If the source is a native Mandarin TTS voice, the result has native pronunciation in your timbre; if the source is you speaking your weaker language, your accent stays. Whether option 2 is still "you" is an ethical choice: disclose it the same way as any synthetic narration (page 9). Inference

Licence traps in the building blocks

A VC tool's MIT licence does not cover the pretrained pieces it downloads. For commercial output, check each one:

  • Content encoders. ContentVec repo is MIT; HuBERT base comes from fairseq (MIT, now archived). Explicit weight licence statements: Unverified.
  • Pitch extractor. The RMVPE weights RVC downloads were trained by a repository with no licence file; another RMVPE implementation is Apache-2.0. Verified (GitHub, 2026-10-17)
  • Training data of pretrained models. Emilia (used by Vevo) is CC BY-NC 4.0. A non-commercial dataset does not automatically make the weights non-commercial, but it is a risk flag. Inference
  • The cleanest documented commercial chain found: Applio/RVC (MIT) + your own data; or MeanVC/MeanVC2 (Apache-2.0 per README); or OpenVoice v2 (MIT including weights per README). Inference
