Glossary

Every speech-AI term used in this guide, in one or two sentences. Type to filter.

TermMeaningWhere used
A/B test (blind)Listeners hear two clips in random order without knowing which tool made which, and pick the better one. The most reliable way to compare voices for your own taste.7, 11
Acoustic modelThe part of a TTS system that turns text (or text tokens) into an intermediate audio representation such as a mel spectrogram or codec tokens.1
Alignment (forced)Given audio and its known transcript, find the start and end time of each word or phoneme. Used for captions and syncing to slides.8
ASRAutomatic speech recognition (speech-to-text), e.g. Whisper, Paraformer, SenseVoice. Used here to check that synthetic speech says what the script says.7, 8
Audio tagsBracketed instructions inside the text, such as [excited] or [whispers], that some models (e.g. ElevenLabs v3/v4) interpret as delivery directions.7
Autoregressive (AR)Generating output one step at a time, each step conditioned on the previous ones, like an LLM writing text. Natural but can repeat or skip.1
C2PA / Content CredentialsAn open standard for attaching signed provenance metadata ("made with tool X") to media files, including audio.9
CERCharacter error rate. Like WER but counted per character; the standard intelligibility metric for Chinese, which has no spaces between words.7
ChunkingSplitting a long script into sentence- or paragraph-sized pieces that are synthesised separately, then joined.1, 8
Code-switchingMixing languages inside one sentence, e.g. English technical terms in Mandarin speech. A distinct capability to test.1, 11
Codec (neural audio codec)A network that compresses audio into discrete integer tokens and back, e.g. SoundStream, EnCodec, DAC. The "tokeniser" of codec language models.1
Codec language modelA transformer that predicts audio-codec tokens from text plus a voice prompt, like an LLM predicting words.1
Cosine similarityA score between −1 and 1 for how closely two embedding vectors point in the same direction. Used for speaker similarity.7
Cross-lingual cloningYour voice speaking a language that your reference audio is not in.1
dBFSDecibels relative to full scale; 0 dBFS is the loudest a digital file can hold. Recording peaks are usually kept well below it.6
De-reverbProcessing that removes room echo from a recording. Over-use causes artefacts that a clone will copy.6
Deep fake (EU AI Act)AI-generated or manipulated image, audio or video that resembles real persons, objects or events and would falsely appear authentic (Art. 3(60)).9
Diffusion modelA generative model that learns to turn noise into data over many denoising steps.1
DriftGradual change of voice, pace or pitch over a long generation, or between sessions.1
Embedding (speaker)A fixed-length vector that summarises who is speaking, independent of the words.1
F0 / pitchFundamental frequency: how high or low the voice is at each moment. Voice conversion tracks and transfers it.4
Fine-tuningFurther training a pretrained model on your recordings so its weights adapt to your voice.1
Flow matchingA generative training method that learns a velocity field moving noise to data in few steps; used by F5-TTS, E2 TTS, CosyVoice's decoder.1
G2PGrapheme-to-phoneme: converting spelling to pronunciation symbols. The source of many mispronunciations.7
Hallucination (TTS)Speech that does not match the text: skipped, repeated, or invented words; mumbling; long silences.7
HuBERT / ContentVecSelf-supervised speech models whose features capture "what is said" while discarding much of "who said it". Used as content encoders in voice conversion.4
Instant voice clone (IVC)Vendor name for zero-shot cloning from a short sample.2
IPAInternational Phonetic Alphabet. Used in SSML <phoneme> tags and pronunciation dictionaries.7
Lexicon / pronunciation dictionaryA list mapping words to how they should be spoken (phonemes or a respelling alias), e.g. "Kubernetes" → "koo-ber-NET-eez".7
Lip-sync avatarA video of a face whose mouth movements are generated to match an audio track.8
LoRALow-rank adaptation: fine-tuning a small set of extra weights instead of the whole model; faster and lighter.3
LUFS / LKFSLoudness units relative to full scale, the perceptual loudness measure in ITU-R BS.1770 and EBU R128. Narration is normalised to a target LUFS.8
Mel spectrogramA time-frequency picture of audio on a perceptual (mel) frequency scale; the classic intermediate representation in TTS.1
MOSMean opinion score: listeners rate naturalness 1–5; the average is the MOS. CMOS compares two systems; SMOS rates speaker similarity.7
Non-autoregressive (NAR)Generating the whole output in parallel rather than step by step (e.g. flow matching). Faster, fewer skipped words.1
Normalisation (text)Rewriting text into speakable words before TTS: "3.5 GB" → "three point five gigabytes", "2026" → "twenty twenty-six".7, 8
Normalisation (loudness)Adjusting gain so every file has the same perceived loudness (target LUFS) and no peaks above a true-peak limit.6, 8
Personal Voice (Azure)Microsoft's limited-access zero-shot voice cloning feature with mandatory consent recording.2
PhonemeThe smallest sound unit that distinguishes words, e.g. /k/ in "cat".7
PinyinRomanised spelling of Mandarin with tone numbers or marks (e.g. hang2); several Chinese-capable models accept it to fix polyphones.7
Polyphone (多音字)A Chinese character with more than one reading depending on context, e.g. 行 xíng / háng.1, 7
Professional voice clone (PVC)Vendor name for a fine-tuned voice trained on longer recordings, usually with identity verification.2
ProsodyThe melody, rhythm, stress and pausing of speech.1
Reference clip / prompt audioThe short recording of you that a zero-shot model imitates.1, 6
Room toneThe quiet background sound of a room; consistent room tone makes joins between chunks inaudible.6, 8
RTFReal-time factor: processing time divided by audio duration. RTF 0.1 means 10 s of compute per 100 s of audio.3
RVCRetrieval-based Voice Conversion, a popular open-source voice-conversion system trained on minutes of a target voice.4
Seed-TTS-evalByteDance's public test set (English and Chinese, plus a "hard" set) for zero-shot TTS, reporting WER/CER and speaker similarity; many papers report on it.3, 7
SNRSignal-to-noise ratio: how much louder your voice is than the background noise, in dB.6
Speaker verificationDeciding whether two clips are the same person. Its models double as similarity scorers, and as bank "voice ID".7, 9
SRT / WebVTTPlain-text subtitle formats with timestamps; generated from word timings.8
SSMLSpeech Synthesis Markup Language: XML tags for pauses (<break>), pronunciation (<phoneme>), rate and pitch (<prosody>). Support varies by vendor and model.7
TimbreThe "colour" of a voice that lets you recognise a person independent of pitch and words.1
True peak (dBTP)The highest level of the reconstructed analogue waveform, which can exceed sample peaks; limited to about −1 dBTP for delivery.8
TTSText-to-speech.everywhere
VisemeThe mouth shape that corresponds to a speech sound; used to drive lip-sync.8
VocoderA model that turns a spectrogram into a waveform, e.g. HiFi-GAN, BigVGAN, Vocos.1
Voice conversion (VC)Speech-to-speech transformation that keeps the words and timing but changes the voice.4
Watermark (audio)An inaudible signal embedded in generated audio that a detector can later find, e.g. SynthID, AudioSeal, PerTh.9
WERWord error rate: (substitutions + deletions + insertions) ÷ words in the reference. Computed by transcribing synthetic audio and comparing with the script.7
Zero-shotCloning a voice from a short sample at inference time, with no training on that voice.1
