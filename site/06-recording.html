Recording and cleaning your voice

The clone can only be as good as the recording. A clone faithfully copies your room echo, your laptop fan, your tired Friday voice, and every artefact a noise remover added. This page is the checklist for getting one clean master recording you can reuse with every tool.

The short version
  1. Record one master set per language: about 60–120 minutes of clean speech each, as 48 kHz, 24-bit, mono WAV. Slice it for each tool. Inference (from the vendor minimums below)
  2. Consistency beats quantity: same room, mic, distance (~20 cm), gain and speaking persona in every session. Verified (ElevenLabs, Azure, Google recording guides)
  3. Read your real teaching material, in your real teaching voice, plus a set of phonetically balanced sentences. Verified (ElevenLabs, Azure)
  4. Clean as little as possible. Fix the room, not the file. Verified (Applio, ElevenLabs docs)

Microphone and placement

USB condenser

Simplest: plug in and record. Picks up the room, so it needs a quiet, soft room. Roughly USD 50–150. Unverified (price class)

XLR condenser + audio interface

Lower noise and more control. ElevenLabs says an XLR mic costing $150–300 is "sufficient for most voiceover work" and names setups like a Focusrite interface with an AT2020 or Rode NT1 at $300–500 total. Verified (ElevenLabs docs; examples, not endorsements)

Dynamic mic

Rejects room sound better, so it is often the better choice in an untreated home office, but needs more gain. Roughly USD 100–400. Practice

Not your laptop or headset mic

Fine for a first zero-shot experiment, not for the master recording. The clone will sound like a laptop. Practice

  • Distance: "about two fists away … approximately 20 cm (7–8 in), with a pop filter"; speaking slightly off-axis reduces plosives. Mark the spot so it is the same every session. Verified (ElevenLabs)
  • Pop filter: required by ElevenLabs, because plosives and breaths "cause issues with the cloning process". Verified

How to reconcile them

Vendors state delivery levels; audio engineers state capture levels. Record at 24-bit with normal lines peaking about −12 to −10 dBFS and the loudest about −6 dBFS, then raise to the vendor's target afterwards. At 24-bit this costs nothing in noise. Inference

The room

  • Deaden reflections: a vocal booth, or a DIY "blanket fort, or closet" (ElevenLabs' words). A small room full of clothes beats a big empty office. Verified (ElevenLabs)
  • Turn off fans and air conditioning, keep the PC away from the mic, record at quiet times.
  • Record 10 seconds of silence (room tone) at the start of every session, for checking the noise floor and for filling gaps later. Practice

Consistency across sessions

This matters more than any single setting. Azure: recordings for one voice "should all sound like they were made on the same day in the same room". ElevenLabs: keep one energy level and one accent; "you can't mix and match or the AI can become unstable". Verified

Google's "match file" method is the most concrete technique: at the start of the project, record a few reference lines; at the start of every later session, listen to them and match volume, energy, cadence and articulation before continuing. Verified (Google custom voice docs)

Professional voice talents manage about 2–3 hours of recording a day, 3–4 days a week (Azure guidance). Plan the master recording over several short sessions. Verified

What to read

Your teaching material first

A clone copies "cadence, tonality, performance style, the length of your pauses … 'uhms' and 'ahs'" (ElevenLabs). So read real lesson scripts, with the English technical terms, numbers and code identifiers you will actually say. Azure suggests mixing general sentences with domain sentences you will use. Verified

English phonetic coverage

  • CMU ARCTIC: 1,132 prompts selected for diphone coverage, from out-of-copyright texts; the licence permits commercial use with attribution. Azure calls it "an excellent starting point". Verified
  • Harvard sentences: 720 sentences in 72 lists of 10. Verified
  • The Rainbow Passage is a common warm-up, but a 2022 ASHA study found it covers only about 36% of English consonant contexts, so it is not truly balanced. Verified

Mandarin phonetic coverage

  • Cover all four tones and the neutral tone, tone sandhi (third-tone pairs, 一 and 不), retroflex versus dental initials (zh/ch/sh versus z/c/s), n versus l, front versus back nasals (-n/-ng), and erhua (儿化) only if you use it consistently. Practice (general phonetics, not from a single source)
  • Include Mandarin sentences with embedded English technical terms, because that is how you will teach. Practice
  • Public Mandarin corpora such as AISHELL-3 are a useful style model, but note the licence conflict: OpenSLR lists Apache-2.0, while AISHELL's own site says research use only and "commercial usage is not allowed". Write your own sentences if commercial. Verified (both pages)

Script hygiene

  • 2–15 seconds per sentence; mostly statements, with some questions and exclamations; no duplicates (Azure). Verified
  • Write numbers and acronyms in the training transcript the way you say them ("nine one one", "A P I"). Verified (Azure)
  • One persona per dataset. If you want a separate "excited" style, record it as a separate set. Verified (Azure, ElevenLabs)

English and Mandarin: record both

ElevenLabs states that the accent comes from the voice samples and that a clone made from English "will very likely have an English accent" in another language. So record a separate set per language. With ElevenLabs Professional cloning this can mean two clones, or one in your main teaching language plus accepting an accent in the other. Azure requires the consent statement in the same language as the training data. Verified

  • Same mic, room, distance and gain for both languages; alternate English and Mandarin blocks within a session so timbre stays matched. Practice
  • Label every file with its language.
  • For voice conversion the source speech sets the accent, so a single target model trained on both languages works (page 4). Inference

Cleaning: as little as possible

The clone learns the artefacts

Applio: "Avoid strong gating, compression, denoising, or reverb removal simply because they are available." ElevenLabs: "You don't need any fancy post-processing, plugins, denoisers." Aggressive noise reduction sounds "watery" or metallic, and the model will reproduce that in every sentence. Always A/B the processed file against the original. Verified

Suggested order

  1. Listen through; delete takes with mistakes, clipping, coughs or other voices.
  2. High-pass filter around 70–80 Hz to remove rumble. Practice
  3. Light noise reduction only if the noise floor misses your target (e.g. SNR > 35 dB).
  4. De-reverb only if the room is clearly audible; better, re-record somewhere deader.
  5. Gentle de-essing and mouth-click removal if needed.
  6. Trim edges to 100–200 ms of silence; slice into 2–15 s clips.
  7. Consistent level per clip (peak normalisation), not aggressive loudness processing.
  8. Transcribe (Whisper for English, FunASR/Paraformer for Chinese) and proofread the transcripts.
  9. Hold a few clips out for evaluation (page 7).

Identity and consent checks vendors require

Every serious vendor makes you prove the voice is yours. Expect to do this once per voice. Full vendor details are on page 2 and the legal background on page 9.

  • Azure Personal Voice: a gated (application-only) service; you record a spoken consent statement in the same language and environment as the voice sample. Verified
  • Google Chirp 3 Instant Custom Voice: a consent clip of up to 10 s recorded in the same environment as the reference. Verified
  • ElevenLabs Professional Voice Clone: you must verify you are the voice owner by reading a prompt; someone else cannot create a PVC of you even with your consent. Instant clones require a checkbox confirmation of rights. Verified (ElevenLabs help centre)
  • Open-source local models: no checks at all. That puts the responsibility, and the security of your recordings, entirely on you. Inference
